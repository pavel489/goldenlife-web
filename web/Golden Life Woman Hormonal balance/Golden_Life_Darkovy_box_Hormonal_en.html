Shatavari (500 mg)

Shatavari (Asparagus racemosus) is a herb from the asparagus family whose root grows wild in the Himalayan foothills and has been harvested in India for thousands of years. In Ayurveda, it is one of the so-called rasayanas – herbs intended for long-term support and renewal of the female body.

Its Sanskrit name is traditionally translated as “she who has a hundred husbands” – a reference to the renowned vitality attributed to it in Ayurvedic tradition.

×

Tribulus terrestris (100 mg)

An unassuming plant that grows from southern Europe through the Middle East to India and China and has appeared in the traditional medicine of both continents for hundreds of years.

In our formula, it has the second-highest dose of all eight extracts and is standardised for saponins, which make up nine tenths of the entire extract (90 mg).

×

Chasteberry (40 mg)

Chasteberry comes from a Mediterranean shrub whose dark berries have been used in European herbal tradition since ancient Greece, and it is also mentioned by Hippocrates.

While Shatavari is a herb of the East, chasteberry is its Western counterpart – the best-known and longest-used herb in European phytotherapy associated with the female cycle.

×

Yarrow (200 mg)

The Latin name for yarrow is Achillea millefolium, and it apparently takes its name from the Greek hero Achilles, who is said to have used it to heal his soldiers' wounds.

In European herbal tradition, yarrow has been associated with women's care for just as long, specifically during the menstrual cycle, where it has a reputation as one of the most reliable local herbs.

×

Red clover (80 mg)

Red clover is among the plants with the very highest natural isoflavone content – plant substances structurally similar to oestrogen.

That is why it is one of the most frequently studied herbs in relation to menopause and is among the standard ingredients in European and North American herbal blends for women.

×

BioPerine® (5 mg)

A patented black pepper extract developed specifically to increase the absorption of other nutrients and active ingredients in the digestive tract (of which 4,75 mg piperine).

Without it, even the best-designed formula could largely pass straight through the body before it had time to use what is inside.
